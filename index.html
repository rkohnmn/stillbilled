Where do I get the file?
  1. Sign in to your bank or card issuer in a web browser.
  2. Open the account and look for Download, Export or Statements. It is usually near the transaction list.
  3. Choose CSV. OFX, QFX and QIF also work. PDF statements cannot be read.
  4. Pick the longest date range offered. Twelve months or more is best, because yearly charges only show up if a year is covered.
  5. Repeat for each account or card, then add all the files together.

Menu names differ between banks and change over time. If your bank offers several CSV layouts, any of them should work. Stillbilled recognises common layouts from Chase, Capital One, American Express and Bank of America, and works out the columns for others.

How it decides

Stillbilled does not know which charges are subscriptions. It looks for a pattern and shows you the evidence, so you can judge.

  • Same merchant, same amount, regular rhythm. Charges are grouped by a cleaned-up merchant name. A group counts as recurring when the amount (within 1% or 10 cents) comes back weekly, every two weeks, monthly, every two months, quarterly, every six months or yearly. A few days either way are allowed for weekends, and one skipped cycle is forgiven.
  • Price increases. The rhythm continues but the amount went up by more than 2% (and at least 20 cents). Stillbilled shows the old price, the new price, and what the difference costs over a year.
  • Possible double charges. The same merchant and exactly the same amount, at least 10 in your currency, within three days, and not part of a regular pattern. If a matching refund shows up within three weeks, it is marked as refunded.
  • Still going or stopped. A charge counts as stopped when its next payment is well overdue as of the newest transaction in your files.

What it cannot do

  • It can be wrong. A gas station you visit every four weeks and pay about the same each time can look like a subscription. Every finding lists the charges behind it, and you can mark anything as ignore.
  • It cannot see inside a merchant. Several subscriptions billed through one name, such as an app store, are separated only when their prices or dates differ.
  • It reads one currency at a time and never converts.
  • It cannot read PDF statements or cancel anything for you.

Privacy, and how to check it

Your statements stay on your device. This page has no server behind it: the files you add are read by JavaScript in this browser tab and never uploaded.

  • The page tells your browser not to let its scripts connect to any server, or load anything from another site. That rule is in the page source, so you can read it.
  • There are no accounts, cookies, analytics, adverts or outside fonts and scripts.
  • The only thing saved in your browser is your light or dark theme choice. Close the tab and your data is gone.
  • A session file you save is a plain list of your transactions. Treat it like the statement itself.

Check it yourself

  1. Open your browser's developer tools and switch to the Network tab.
  2. Add a file or use the sample data. No new requests appear.
  3. Or turn off your internet connection after the page has loaded. It keeps working.

The page itself is delivered by its host like any website, so the host can see that someone loaded it. It never sees your files.
